API reference¶

Cumulant generating functions and the high-level interface¶

class saddlepoint_expansions.CGF(K, d, domain=None, taylor=None)¶

A cumulant generating function K of a random vector of dimension d.

K is a callable K(t) for a numpy vector t of length d (also when d = 1: write t[0]). It must be written generically, with arithmetic and numpy functions (np.log, np.exp, np.sqrt, powers, np.sum, products with constant matrices), because it is evaluated on vectors of truncated Taylor polynomials to generate its derivatives. Avoid math.log and the like, float(...) conversions, and library calls that require floats. Outside the domain it may raise a DomainError (np.log and np.sqrt of a negative constant term do this automatically) or return a non-finite value.

domain is an optional predicate t -> bool for the domain of K. It is needed when the formula is finite outside the true domain (1/(1 - t) for t > 1), and it is cheaper than an exception.

taylor supplies the Taylor polynomials in closed form instead of by automatic differentiation: a function taylor(t, N, M) returning the polynomial of degree N in v of K(t + M v) as a TPoly (M = None for the identity; M may be rectangular), or None outside the domain; build it from a coefficient table with from_coefficients(). The library uses it where closed forms are faster (quadratic_forms_cgf()), and the transformations carry it along.

A CGF is callable. See derivatives(), saddlepoint(), tailprob(), density(), and the transformations reflect(), marginal(), linear_combination(), sample_mean(), shift() and K1 + K2 (sum of independent vectors).

Example:

K = CGF(lambda t: -3*np.log(1 - t[0]) - 2*np.log(1 - t[1]) - np.log(1 - t[0] - t[1]), 2,
        domain=lambda t: t[0] < 1 and t[1] < 1 and t[0] + t[1] < 1)
saddlepoint_expansions.derivatives(K, t, N)¶

All derivatives K^(nu)(t) of orders |nu| <= N, as a dict keyed by the multi-index tuple (D[(0, 0)] is K(t), D[(1, 0)] is dK/dt_1, …), or None when t is outside the domain.

saddlepoint_expansions.saddlepoint(K, x, tau0=None)¶

The saddlepoint K'(tau) = x by Newton’s method with backtracking (see spa_saddle()). Returns (tau, info); for d = 1, x may be a scalar and tau is then a scalar.

saddlepoint_expansions.tailprob(K, x, order=3, lower=False, signs=None, **kwargs)¶

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1..order of the orthant probability P(X >= x) (componentwise) for the random vector with cumulant generating function K.

Returns a dict with F (array over the orders; F[M-1] keeps the terms of orders n^(-m/2), m <= 2M-1, and has relative error O(n^-M) uniformly for saddlepoints in compact subsets of the closed positive orthant, Theorem 1 of the paper; F[0] is the approximation of Kolassa (2003) in two or more dimensions), tau (the saddlepoint), status and info (the full result of the underlying routine).

lower=True gives P(X <= x), and signs (a vector of +-1) any mixed corner, e.g. signs=[1, -1] for P(X1 >= x1, X2 <= x2). When the saddlepoint has negative components the expansion is applied through the inclusion-exclusion identity of the paper. In three dimensions the orthant integrals are computed by Gauss quadrature and in four or more by randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (info['err'] is then a standard error); keyword arguments are passed to spa_mv() (nG, nqmc, …) and, for d = 2, to spa_orthant2() (nG, logscale, …). For d = 1, x may be a scalar.

saddlepoint_expansions.density(K, x, order=3, **kwargs)¶

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1..order of the density at x (Theorem 3 of the paper; Daniels’s expansion when d = 1): f[M-1] keeps the terms m <= M-1 and has relative error O(n^-M). A finite sum of Hermite polynomials, no integration. Returns a dict with f, tau, status and info. In three or more dimensions the cancellation indicator info['kappa_pdf'] should be checked at high orders (the Julia package repeats the computation in higher precision when it is large; this package works in double precision).

saddlepoint_expansions.ratio_density(K, r, order=3, general=False)¶

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1..order of the joint density at r of the vector of ratios R = (X_1/Y_1, ..., X_d/Y_d), d = len(r), with positive denominators. K is the cgf of (X_1..X_d, Y_1..Y_d) (dimension 2d) or, for a common denominator Y, of (X_1..X_d, Y) (dimension d + 1). For a scalar r (one ratio, K of dimension two) the expansion is the Rice expansion through the saddlepoint of K_W(s) = K(s, -r s) (Theorem 4 of the paper); for a vector it is Theorem 4 with the amplitude expansion of Corollary 1 in whitened coordinates, every Taylor coefficient taken from one Taylor polynomial of K(s, -r*s + tau) (ratio_mv()). general=True uses the vector code also for d = 1. Returns a dict with f, shat, status and info.

saddlepoint_expansions.ratio_tailprob(K, r, order=3, lower=False, signs=None, **kwargs)¶

Saddlepoint expansions of orders 1..order of P(R >= r) for the vector of ratios R = (X_1/Y_1, ..., X_d/Y_d) with positive denominators (K as in ratio_density(); a scalar r for one ratio): the probability equals P(W >= 0) for W = X - r*Y, and tailprob() is applied to W at 0 (Theorem 4 of the paper). lower=True gives P(R <= r), signs a mixed corner, and the other keyword arguments are passed to tailprob. Returns a dict with F, shat, status and info.

saddlepoint_expansions.reflect(K, signs=None)¶

Cumulant generating function of (s_1 X_1, ..., s_d X_d) for signs +-1: K(s * t). reflect(K) negates every component.

saddlepoint_expansions.marginal(K, idx)¶

Cumulant generating function of the components idx of X.

saddlepoint_expansions.linear_combination(K, B)¶

Cumulant generating function of Y = B'X for a d x k matrix B: K(B u).

saddlepoint_expansions.sample_mean(K, n)¶

Cumulant generating function of the mean of n independent copies: n K(t/n).

saddlepoint_expansions.shift(K, c)¶

Cumulant generating function of X + c: K(t) + c't.

Library of cumulant generating functions¶

A library of cumulant generating functions, written generically for Taylor-mode differentiation.

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.normal_cgf(mu, Sigma)¶

N(mu, Sigma): K(t) = mu't + t'Sigma t/2. The expansions are exact at order one.

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.gamma_cgf(a, b=1.0)¶

Gamma distribution with shape a and scale b (mean ab): K(t) = -a log(1 - bt), t < 1/b. gamma_cgf(1, b) is the exponential distribution with mean b.

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.ncx2_cgf(m, lam=0.0)¶

Noncentral chi-squared chi^2_m(lam): K(t) = -(m/2) log(1 - 2t) + lam t/(1 - 2t), t < 1/2.

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.exponential_sums_cgf(A, n=1.0, shift=None)¶

The mean of n independent copies of A'U + l, U a vector of independent unit exponential variables, A an m x d matrix and shift = l (default 0): K(t) = -n sum_i log(1 - a_i't/n) + l't, domain a_i't < n. With n = 1 this is the cumulant generating function of A'U + l itself (the examples of Wang 1990 and Kolassa and Li 2010 in the paper).

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.quadratic_forms_cgf(Mats, Sigma=None, mu=None, scale=1.0, closedform=True)¶

Joint cumulant generating function of the quadratic forms Q_i = z'A_i z, Mats = [A_1, ..., A_d] (symmetric p x p), in z ~ N(mu, Sigma) (default standard normal): K(t) = -1/2 log det(I - 2C) + nu'C(I - 2C)^-1 nu, C = sum_i t_i S^1/2 A_i S^1/2, nu = S^-1/2 mu, multiplied by scale (the sum of scale independent copies when scale is an integer). The domain is where I - 2C is positive definite. The determinant and the solve use a Cholesky factorization in generic arithmetic; the cost of one evaluation with derivatives is about p^3/6 polynomial products, so this is for small and moderate p. With closedform=True (the default) the Taylor polynomials, including the whitened ones, are instead computed in closed form by the trace recursion of the paper in the whitened directions (the taylor option of CGF), so that large p costs matrix products rather than polynomial products; closedform=False differentiates the generic formula.

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.wishart_diag_cgf(V, n)¶

The diagonal of a Wishart matrix W ~ W_d(n, V): d quadratic forms z'e_i e_i'z in z ~ N(0, V), summed over n independent copies (quadratic_forms_cgf() with scale = n).

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.common_component_cgf(m, lam=None)¶

Q_j = X_j + X_0, j = 1..d, with independent X_i ~ chi^2_{m_i}(lam_i), m = [m_1, ..., m_d, m_0] and lam likewise: K(t) = sum_j K_j(t_j) + K_0(sum_j t_j) (the pairs of quadratic forms of the paper, and the trivariate example).

saddlepoint_expansions.cgfs.uniform_cgf(a=-1.0, b=1.0)¶

Uniform distribution on [a, b]: K(t) = log sinhc((b - a)t/2) + (a + b)t/2, with sinhc(x) = sinh(x)/x (sinhc(), which handles the removable singularity at t = 0, where the saddlepoint iteration starts). Finite for all t.

The derivative interface¶

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_saddle(Kfun, x, tau0=None)¶

Saddlepoint K'(tau) = x in any dimension: Newton’s method with backtracking on the convex objective K(tau) - tau'x, convergence measured by the Newton decrement (see the Julia package for the stopping rules). A step is rejected when the objective falls below its tangent plane at the current iterate, when the directional derivative decreases along the step, or when the midpoint of the step is outside the domain: on the strip the objective is convex, so it lies above its tangent plane and its gradient is monotone, and a violation means the step crossed a pole of K into a region where its formula happens to be finite (-log|1 - t| beyond t = 1, where the derivative changes sign). Necessary conditions, which protect cumulant generating functions given without a domain predicate in the usual cases; the predicate remains the safe way. Kfun(nu, t) returns the derivative of order nu (a tuple) at t, NaN outside the domain. Returns (tau, info).

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_uni(Kd, shat, xbar, order, uthr=2.5)¶

Saddlepoint approximations of orders 1..order of the density and the distribution function of a scalar variable at xbar from the derivatives of its cumulant generating function at the saddlepoint: Kd[k] = K^(k)(shat), k = 0..2*order+1.

Returns a dict with pdf (Daniels expansion with the terms m <= M-1), cdf (Hauschildt-Robinson expansion of P(X <= xbar) with the terms m <= 2M-1), upp (the same expansion of P(X > xbar), computed directly) and kappa (cancellation indicator), each an array over the orders. Both signs of shat are handled. The integrals I_m(u), u = shat K''^(1/2), are generated by the forward recursion for |u| <= uthr and by 80-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature otherwise.

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_ratio_pdf(KWd, gd, shat, order)¶

Rice (Laplace) expansion of orders 1..order of (2 pi i)^-1 int exp{K_W(s)} g(s) ds through the saddlepoint shat of K_W (K_W'(shat) = 0): KWd[k] = K_W^(k)(shat), k = 0..2*order, and gd[l] = g^(l)(shat), l = 0..2*order-2. For the density of R = X/Y at r with Y > 0 and joint cumulant generating function K(s, t), take K_W(s) = K(s, -r s) and g(s) = dK/dt at t = -r s.

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_bi(Kfun, x, order, tau0=None, nG=200, Kwhite=None, direct=False, logscale=0.0, qtol=1e-08, nGmax=None)¶

Bivariate saddlepoint approximations of orders 1..order at x = [x1, x2] from Kfun(nu, t), the derivative of order nu (a tuple) of the cumulant generating function at t (NaN outside the domain). See spa_bi_at() for the result and the keywords; Kwhite(nu, u, T) optionally returns the derivatives of K_Y(u) = K(T u).

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_bi_at(Kd, tau, x, order, nG=200, Kwhite=None, direct=False, logscale=0.0, qtol=1e-08, nGmax=None)¶

The bivariate expansions from the derivatives at the saddlepoint tau: Kd[n1, n2] = K^(n1,n2)(tau), n1 + n2 <= 2*order + 1.

Returns a dict with pdf[M] (density expansion with the terms m <= M-1), Fup[M] (P(X1 >= x1, X2 >= x2) with the terms m <= 2M-1; NaN when the saddlepoint has a negative component unless direct), canc and kappa (cancellation indicators), qerr (relative change of Fup with half the quadrature nodes; the number of nodes is doubled while qerr[-1] > qtol, up to nGmax, default 4 nG), status and nG. logscale = c scales Fup by exp(-c). The terms are evaluated for the whitened vector Y = L^-1 X, K'' = L L'; the Taylor coefficients of K_Y come from Kwhite(nu, u, T) (T = L^-T) when given and by polynomial substitution otherwise.

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_orthant2(Kfun, x, order, nG=200, Kwhite=None, logscale=0.0, qtol=1e-08)¶

P(X1 >= x1, X2 >= x2) of orders 1..order by the bivariate expansion, with the inclusion-exclusion when the saddlepoint has negative components; the one-dimensional terms use spa_uni(). With logscale = c every term is scaled by exp(-c). Returns a dict with Fup, tau, canc, kappa, status (those of the bivariate term of the highest order).

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_mv(Kfun, x, order, tau0=None, method='auto', Kwhite=None, **kwargs)¶

Saddlepoint approximations of orders 1..order in dimension d = len(x) of the density and of the orthant probability P(X >= x) from Kfun(nu, t), the derivative of order nu (a tuple) of the cumulant generating function at t (NaN outside the domain); see spa_mv_at().

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_mv_at(Kd, tau, x, order, method='auto', nG=64, nGH=48, nqmc=16384, nshift=8, seed=1, Kwhite=None, qtol=None, nGmax=None)¶

The expansions from the derivatives at a given saddlepoint: Kd is a dict from multi-indices (tuples) to K^(nu)(tau), |nu| <= 2*order + 1.

Returns a dict with pdf[M] (density expansion with the terms m <= M-1), Fup[M] (P(X >= x) with the terms m <= 2M-1; NaN when the saddlepoint has a negative component, use spa_orthant()), kappa and kappa_pdf (cancellation indicators), err (quadrature check: the change with half the nodes for method='quad', the standard error over the random shifts for 'qmc'), method_cdf, nodes and status. method is 'quad' (Gauss quadrature, d = 3), 'qmc' (randomized quasi-Monte Carlo, any d), 'none' (density only) or 'auto' ('quad' for d = 3, 'qmc' otherwise). The terms are evaluated in whitened coordinates; the Taylor coefficients of K_Y(u) = K(T u) come from Kwhite(nu, u, T) when given and by polynomial substitution otherwise.

saddlepoint_expansions.spa_orthant(Kfun, x, order, Kwhite=None, **kwargs)¶

P(X >= x) of orders 1..order in any dimension, with the inclusion-exclusion of the paper when the saddlepoint has negative components: every term is the orthant probability of a marginal with some components negated, computed by spa_uni() (one dimension), spa_bi() (two) or spa_mv() (three or more), recursively. Returns a dict with Fup, tau and err (the sum of the absolute error estimates of the terms; NaN when a term has none). Kwhite is transformed for every term (it must accept a rectangular d x k matrix).

saddlepoint_expansions.orthant2(t, S, rtol=1e-13)¶

P(Z_1 >= t_1, Z_2 >= t_2), Z ~ N(0, S), by one-dimensional adaptive quadrature.

saddlepoint_expansions.orthant3(t, S, rtol=1e-13)¶

P(Z >= t), Z ~ N(0, S) trivariate, by adaptive quadrature over z_1 of the bivariate conditional orthant probability.

saddlepoint_expansions.kfun(K, maxdeg)¶

Kfun(nu, t) for the derivative interface, from a CGF: the Taylor polynomial at the last t is memoized (degree 2 while only derivatives of order <= 2 are requested, as in the saddlepoint iteration, otherwise maxdeg). Not thread-safe: one per task.

saddlepoint_expansions.kwhite(K, maxdeg)¶

Kwhite(nu, u, M) for the derivative interface: derivatives at u of K_Y(u) = K(M u) from the Taylor polynomial of K(M u + M v), which the expansions evaluate in whitened coordinates; M may be rectangular. Memoized at the last (u, M).

saddlepoint_expansions.ratio_mv(K2, r, order)¶

Saddlepoint expansion of orders 1..order of the joint density at r of R_j = X_j/Y_j from the cgf K2 of (X_1..X_d, Y_1..Y_d), Y_j > 0: with W = X - r*Y, K_W(s) = K(s, -r*s), K_W'(shat) = 0, K_W'' = L L', f(r) = (2 pi i)^-d int exp(K_W(s)) g(s) ds with g the sum over the partitions of the index set of the products of the mixed derivatives K_B(s, -r*s), expanded in whitened coordinates (Corollary 1 of the paper). Returns (f, info).

The Taylor algebra¶

Truncated multivariate Taylor polynomials (Taylor-mode automatic differentiation).

A TPoly is a polynomial in d variables truncated at total degree N, with float64 coefficients stored in the graded monomial order of its Algebra. The arithmetic operations and the elementary functions (exp, log, sqrt, powers, division, the trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, arctan, and sincu/sinhc for sin(x)/x and sinh(x)/x with their removable singularity) propagate the coefficients exactly (up to rounding), so that evaluating a function written in ordinary arithmetic on the variables of an algebra returns its Taylor polynomial, whose coefficient of u^nu is the derivative of order nu divided by nu!.

Numpy ufuncs work on TPoly objects (np.log(p), np.exp(p), np.sqrt(p), and the arithmetic ufuncs), also elementwise on object arrays of them, so that a cumulant generating function written with numpy for float arguments usually works unchanged.

exception saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.DomainError¶

Raised by log, sqrt and non-integer powers of a polynomial whose constant term is outside the domain of the function (negative, or zero for log).

saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.get_algebra(d, N)¶

The algebra of d variables truncated at degree N (cached).

class saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.Algebra(d, N)¶

Index tables of the truncated algebra of d variables and total degree N.

variables()¶

The d variables as polynomials.

class saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.TPoly(alg, c)¶

A truncated multivariate Taylor polynomial; see the module docstring.

coeff(nu)¶

The coefficient of u^nu (nu a tuple), which is f^(nu)(0)/nu!.

derivative(nu)¶

The derivative of order nu at the expansion point: nu! * coeff(nu).

sincu()¶

sin(x)/x (unnormalized), with the removable singularity at a zero constant term.

sinhc()¶

sinh(x)/x, with the removable singularity at a zero constant term.

saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.sincu(x)¶

sin(x)/x (unnormalized, unlike np.sinc), equal to 1 at 0; works on floats, arrays and polynomials, where the removable singularity at a zero constant term is handled.

saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.sinhc(x)¶

sinh(x)/x, equal to 1 at 0; works on floats, arrays and polynomials, where the removable singularity at a zero constant term is handled. The cumulant generating function of the uniform distribution on [-1, 1] is log(sinhc(t)).

saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.from_coefficients(d, N, coeffs)¶

A polynomial of degree N in d variables from a table of its coefficients: a dict from exponent tuples to the coefficient of u^nu (f^(nu)/nu!), missing entries zero. For closed-form taylor functions of a CGF.

saddlepoint_expansions.taylor.variables(d, N)¶

The d variables of the algebra of degree N, as a numpy object array.
